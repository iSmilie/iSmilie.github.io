Age of the Universe

The Universe, whose current age is \(t \sim 10^{10}\) years, is filled with equilibrium relic radiation at a temperature \(T \approx 3\) K. Starting from the epoch when the temperature was \(T_0 \sim 3000\) K and neutral atoms were formed, the radiation interacted only weakly with matter and expanded adiabatically together with the Universe. Estimate the age of the Universe at the moment when neutral atoms were formed. Assume that the expansion rate of the Universe is constant.

From the statement it is known that the process is adiabatic: \[ T V^{\gamma - 1} = \text{const}, \] which implies \[ T \sim \frac{1}{V^{\gamma - 1}}. \] The gas in the Universe is considered to be relativistic (radiation-dominated): \[ \gamma = \frac{4}{3}. \] It follows that the temperature of the Universe is inversely proportional to its linear size: \[ T \sim \frac{1}{(a^3)^{1/3}} \Rightarrow T \sim \frac{1}{a}. \] Assuming a constant expansion velocity, we have \[ a = vt, \] from which it follows that the temperature is inversely proportional to the age of the Universe: \[ T \sim \frac{1}{t}. \] Therefore, \[ T_0 t_0 = T t, \] and hence \[ \boxed{t_0 = t \frac{T}{T_0} = 10^7 \text{ years}}. \]

Random wandering

A man leaves a tavern and wanders randomly. Each of his steps has a length \(L\), but the direction of each step is random. On average, how far from the tavern will he be after a long walk of \(N\) steps?

The vector of a single step directed at a random angle \(\alpha_i\), uniformly distributed in \([0;2\pi)\), to the \(x\)-axis is \[ \vec{L}=L(\cos\alpha_i\hat{x}+\sin\alpha_i\hat{y}). \] The displacement vector is given by the sum of the step vectors: \[ \vec{S}=L\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\cos\alpha_i\hat{x}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin\alpha_i\hat{y}\right). \] Therefore, the squared displacement is \[ S^2=L^2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\cos^2\alpha_i+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sin^2\alpha_i +2\sum_{j>i}\sin\alpha_i\sin\alpha_j+2\sum_{j>i}\cos\alpha_i\cos\alpha_j\right). \] Averaging, and noting that the mean value of a sum equals the sum of the mean values of its terms, we obtain \[ \langle S^2\rangle=L^2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\cos^2\alpha_i\rangle +\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\sin^2\alpha_i\rangle +2\sum_{j>i}\langle\cos(\alpha_i-\alpha_j)\rangle\right). \] Hence, \[ \langle S^2\rangle=NL^2. \] Thus, the root-mean-square displacement is \[ \boxed{S_{\text{rms}}=L\sqrt{N}}. \] Remark. What has been computed above is the root-mean-square displacement (rms). If instead one is interested in the mean value of the magnitude of the displacement vector, then for large \(N\) the central limit theorem can be used. The displacement components are \[ S_x = L\sum_{i=1}^N \cos\alpha_i,\qquad S_y = L\sum_{i=1}^N \sin\alpha_i, \] which have zero means and variances \(\langle S_x^2\rangle=\langle S_y^2\rangle=\dfrac{1}{2}NL^2\). In the limit of large \(N\), they are normally distributed. Therefore, the magnitude \[ S = \sqrt{S_x^2+S_y^2} \] follows the Rayleigh distribution with parameter \(\sigma^2=\dfrac{1}{2}NL^2\). For the Rayleigh distribution, it is known that \[ \langle S\rangle = \sigma\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}. \] Substituting \(\sigma = \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}NL^2}\), we obtain \[ \langle |S|\rangle \approx \sqrt{\frac{NL^2}{2}}\,\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\,L\sqrt{N}\approx 0.886\,L\sqrt{N}. \] Thus, for large \(N\), \[ \boxed{\;\;\langle |S|\rangle \;\approx\; 0.886\,L\sqrt{N}\;}. \]
